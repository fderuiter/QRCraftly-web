# Optical ceiling benchmark

Generated by `pnpm run bench:optical-ceiling --write`. Every number comes from the channel simulator in `src/packages/optical-modem`, a model of a phone camera, and not from any device. Real-device numbers come from the probe page ([ADR 0027](adr/0027-optical-channel-probe.md)) and the device checklist ([TRANSFER_DEVICE_CHECKLIST.md](TRANSFER_DEVICE_CHECKLIST.md)); until then they are unmeasured.

The question it answers: can a camera that sees the whole screen carry 32 Mbit/s (4 MB/s) of file data? The QR transfer that ships reaches 135 KB/s at 30 fps and 273 KB/s at 60 fps in its own simulation ([TRANSFER_BENCHMARK.md](TRANSFER_BENCHMARK.md)); the modem ladder's densest profile reaches 81 KB/s ([OPTICAL_LADDER_BENCHMARK.md](OPTICAL_LADDER_BENCHMARK.md)), because its grid fills only a corner of a 1080p picture.

## Profiles

Grids sized so a cell covers about 4 camera pixels when the screen fills the simulator's framing (five sixths of the picture's width). Each block carries one symbol of the outer code, so a frame needs no per-block header: the symbol index is the frame number times the blocks per frame plus the block number. Every block also carries the 4-byte identity tag of ADR 0043.

## Transfers

A seeded random file of 10 frames' worth of data through the outer code, one camera frame per frame sent. "Lock" is the camera frame the receiver first read; every later frame was looked for near the last one (the tracked search). "Blocks repaired" counts from the lock on. Goodput is the file's bytes over the camera time to the frame that completed it, lock-on included, after the rebuilt file matched the sent one byte for byte. A short file pays for the lock-on frame and the outer code's last symbols, so "steady rate" adds the data in repaired blocks per second from the lock on, the rate a long file approaches (before the outer code's overhead, about 0.1% in [TRANSFER_BENCHMARK.md](TRANSFER_BENCHMARK.md)). In the studio, typical and poor channels the simulated phone does not move between frames; the tracked search's tolerance of movement is tested in `src/packages/optical-modem/tests/tracking.test.ts`. The phone channel is the typical one plus guessed lens distortion, rolling-shutter shear, stabiliser jitter of up to 2 pixels, OLED PWM bands, edge enhancement, chroma and temporal noise reduction, and a screen refresh in every capture whose tear line drifts down the picture (`phoneImpairments` in the script). Its settings are guesses until the probe measures a phone (#1173); blocks below the tear belong to the next frame, and the receiver reads them as that frame's blocks and finishes a frame from two consecutive captures ([ADR 0049](adr/0049-optical-modem-torn-capture-recovery.md)). A block read twice counts once.

## Decode time

Median wall time per camera frame on this machine, one thread, the modem module in WebAssembly under Node, sampling on the CPU (the browser receiver can sample on the GPU, ADR 0029). "Full" is a frame found by the whole-image fiducial search, "tracked" one found near the last. "Threads at 30 / 60 fps" is how many such threads it would take to keep up, if frames are shared between workers. "Outer code" is the time spent adding the repaired blocks to the outer decoder over the whole transfer.

## What this does not show

- A phone. Lens fall-off, focus across a wide grid, glare, exposure and the camera pipeline's own processing are only modelled, and the cells are smaller than anything the probe has measured.
- That the camera delivers the frame rate. Browsers often deliver 4K at 30 fps, and some cameras not at all; the 60 fps column needs a camera and browser that hand over 60 full frames a second.
- That the display shows every frame. A 60 Hz screen that drops or tears frames costs blocks; tearing is in the simulator but not in these rows.
- That a phone decodes in time: the thread counts above are for a desktop CPU.
- How long Ultra Mono takes to lock on while the phone is being aimed. Black and white is the finder's slowest case: with a fiducial out of view a 4K frame takes about 1.3 s ([ADR 0047](adr/0047-optical-modem-tracked-search-and-high-rate-profiles.md)).
